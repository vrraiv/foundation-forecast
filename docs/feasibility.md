sample-file descriptions are in `docs/probes/` (statcan.md, ecb_eurostat.md, oecd_misc.md,
models.md). This document summarises, marks every section-3 row of PLAN.md as
**confirmed / changed / unavailable**, and recommends a final target list, window and
origin rule for HUMAN GATE 0. Nothing here is a decision; decisions are the owner's.

Status vocabulary: *confirmed* = verified by a direct pull or primary document;
*confirmed (mirror)* = verified only through a third-party copy; *changed* = exists but
differs from the plan; *unavailable* = not found in any public source we checked;
*not verified* = could not be tested in this probe.

## 3. Three timing definitions the owner must fix

These determine what "no look-ahead" means in code and are not derivable from the plan.

1. **Publication date of an OECD edition.** Editions are stamped with a month only. Observed content implies the snapshot is taken in the first half of the month (edition `M` never contains Canadian CPI for `M-1`, which StatCan releases on the 15th-22nd, and contains LFS for `M-1` only sometimes). Options: (a) conservative, edition `M` becomes visible at the first origin after month `M` ends; (b) calendar-aligned, each observation becomes visible on its known national release date, with the edition used only for its values. Cost of (a): the newest Canadian unemployment observation in the information set is for month `M-1` or `M-2` of an edition that becomes visible in month `M+1`, so the model sees data one to two releases (roughly 4 to 8 weeks) older than a human forecaster had at the same origin; CPI and retail are similarly 1 to 2 releases behind. This changes what horizon 1 means for those targets, and the live registry must either mimic the same staleness or diverge from the backtest. Recommendation: (a) as primary, (b) as a Phase 2 refinement where release calendars are reliable.
2. **Releases on the origin day.** StatCan CPI and retail trade fall on the 20th-or-next-business-day origin in 5 of 14 origins checked and within one day in 5 more; euro-area full HICP falls on the 17th-24th. Vintage dates are day-granular, so an intraday rule cannot be enforced in the backtest. Options: (a) information set = everything with vintage date strictly before the origin date; (b) move the origin to a day with no collisions. Checked ranges: Canadian CPI 14th-22nd, Canadian retail 19th-24th, euro-area full HICP 17th-24th; monthly and quarterly GDP fall on the last business day; LFS first or second Friday; euro-area unemployment and GDP flash at month end, retail first week, IP mid-month. In the twelve months of Canadian release dates observed (Nov 2025 to Nov 2026), **a 25th-or-next-business-day origin would include CPI, retail trade and full HICP every month**; the monthly and quarterly GDP releases fall after it in every month except December 2025, when monthly GDP came out on the 23rd. The only target or conditioning release on a 25th in that window is SEPH payrolls (a conditioning series) on 2026-06-25. The euro-area side rests on one observed month plus the ECB's HICP calendar and is **not verified** for retail, IP, unemployment or GDP. Recommendation: origin on the 25th, information set strictly before the origin date (local time of the statistical office), to be re-checked against a full euro-area calendar in Phase 2.
3. **"First release" when the source is a snapshot.** RTD has 8 vintages a year and OECD 12; the first value seen may already have been revised once by the statistical office. Recommendation: define "first release" as the value in the earliest vintage containing the period, and record the vintage lag per observation so the scoring can report how often it exceeds one release cycle.

## 5. Recommendations for Gate 0

**Targets (per economy).** Keep: real GDP (Q), headline inflation (M; vintage-free with lag enforcement for Canada), unemployment rate (M), retail sales volume (M), household final consumption (Q; replacing monthly GDP by industry for Canada and kept for the euro area alongside industrial production). Core inflation: no vintage source in either economy; recommend keeping it as a lag-only series excluded from the Q1 comparison, or dropping it. Euro-area industrial production and retail: recommend OECD editions as the single vintage source for these two targets in both economies, so the two economies are treated alike, with RTD used as a precision cross-check to Oct 2025.

**Vintage sources.** Precise-dated sources where live (StatCan GDP tables, RTD for GDP, HICP, unemployment, consumption), OECD editions elsewhere. Record the source and timing convention per observation. Prefer dropping to splicing where a series has two conventions.

**Window.** The 2015 rationale no longer binds: most Canadian monthly targets rest on OECD editions whatever the start date, and RTD covers 2001+ via the API. What does bind is in-vintage history for the benchmarks. In the January 2001 OECD edition, Canada has 159 quarters (GDP, consumption, from 1961) and 478-491 months (CPI, unemployment, IP, retail, from 1960-61). In the March 2001 EA18 edition, euro-area GDP has only **23 quarters** (1995Q1 to 2000Q3), retail 72 months (from 1995), unemployment 127, CPI 133, IP 192. A BVAR or factor model on 23 quarters is not credible, and euro-area GDP reaches 40 in-vintage quarters only around 2005. Recommendation: keep **2015-01 to present as the primary window** (plan default) and pre-register **2001-01 to 2014-12 as an extension** for every target whose in-vintage history at the origin is at least 10 years (all Canadian targets, euro-area monthly targets; euro-area quarterly targets from about 2005). Report results with and without 2020Q1 to 2021Q2. RTD in-vintage depth at 2001 was not measured in this probe. Sample sizes will still be small; report them with every statistic.

**Origin.** 25th or next business day, information set strictly before the origin date (see section 3). If the owner prefers the 20th, adopt rule 2(a) and accept that the latest CPI is in the set in some months and not others.

**Canadian data route.** Scripted access to StatCan is blocked from this machine. Options: (a) owner opens `https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/wds/rest/getCodeSets` in a browser to confirm the block is bot-detection only; (b) accept the DBnomics mirror for the three GDP tables, Valet for CPI, and OECD editions for everything else, with StatCan itself used only when reachable; (c) test from another network in Phase 1. Under (b) the live registry has no same-day feed for Canadian unemployment or retail sales. We will not engineer around the Cloudflare challenge.

**Repository visibility.** The planning docs and these probe reports are now on the public GitHub remote, as the owner allowed. PLAN.md Gate 5 anticipates a private repository for code and forecasts and a public one for the ledger only. The owner should decide whether code stays public or the repository is made private before Phase 1 adds code.

**Models.** Keep the four planned models. Flag Moirai 2.0's non-commercial licence and TiRex's community licence for the owner's own assessment. Recommend adding TiRex-2 as a fifth model (Apache-2.0, second multivariate model); note its macro exposure via FRED-MD when interpreting Q2. Exclude TimesFM 3.0.

**Open items carried forward from the first response.** OpenTimestamps stubbed in the registry dry run (recommended); GPU not used (recommended); the quarterly frequency of the consumer-spending target (owner's call).
